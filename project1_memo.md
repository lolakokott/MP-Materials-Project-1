# MP Materials — Investment Committee Memo

**Target:** MP Materials Corp. (NYSE: MP)  
**Recommendation:** WATCH / DEFER  
**Valuation reference date:** September 10, 2026  
**Historical base:** FY2025  
**Forecast period:** 2026–2030  
**Currency:** USD

Base enterprise value: −$1,108.79M
Downside enterprise value: −$1,244.15M
Upside enterprise value: −$973.43M
WACC: 11%
Terminal growth: 3%
Historical reference stock price: $56.77

## Investment Recommendation

I recommend that the fund defer initiating a position in MP Materials. Although the company has significant growth opportunities in domestic rare-earth processing and magnet manufacturing, its capital requirements, operating losses, and uncertain future profitability make the investment difficult to justify under my current assumptions.

## Financial Analysis

My integrated five-year pro-forma forecasts MP Materials' income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement. These forecasts drive an FCFF discounted cash flow model using an 11% WACC and 3% terminal growth assumption.

The model produces the following preliminary enterprise values:

| Scenario | Enterprise Value |
|---|---:|
| Downside | -$1,244.15M |
| Base | -$1,108.79M |
| Upside | -$973.43M |

The downside case fails the modeled minimum-cash requirement in 2030 and therefore is not a financially feasible scenario without additional funding.

The base and upside cases pass the existing accounting and liquidity checks, but their negative modeled enterprise values indicate that the forecast cash outflows exceed the discounted terminal operating value.

These are diagnostic enterprise-value results, not a defensible common-share valuation range. A positive per-share valuation has not been established under these scenarios.

## Comparable Company Analysis

I considered Energy Fuels and Idaho Strategic Resources as potential peers. Idaho Strategic Resources produced a P/S multiple of 9.52x and EV/Revenue multiple of 8.14x.

However, IDR's primary gold-mining business differs substantially from MP Materials' rare-earth processing and magnet manufacturing operations. Additionally, MP's negative earnings make a conventional P/E valuation unsuitable.

I therefore use comparables as secondary market evidence rather than averaging them mechanically with the DCF.

## Reverse DCF

Using a historical reference price of $56.77, the simplified reverse DCF indicates that an approximately 663% terminal operating margin would be necessary under the model's other assumptions.

This is not a realistic operating expectation. However, the calculation is limited by differences in financial statement dates and simplified capital-structure treatment. It demonstrates the sensitivity of valuation to operating assumptions rather than proving the stock is overvalued.

## Risks and Monitoring Triggers

The main risks are high capital expenditures, delays in expanding production, dependence on government agreements, commodity-price volatility, and potential shareholder dilution.

I would reconsider the recommendation if MP demonstrates sustained improvements in operating profitability, provides credible evidence of manufacturing capacity increases, and establishes sufficient financing to support its expansion plans.

## Conclusion

MP Materials has long-term strategic opportunities, but the current model does not provide sufficient evidence to support initiating an investment.

I recommend placing the company on watch and reassessing its valuation as operating performance, capital spending, and financing conditions become clearer.

Stronger operating margins and evidence of production growth would lead me to change my mind.

## Limitations

The DCF uses a simplified FY2025 financial base and historical 2026 market evidence. The model also assumes a substantial transition to mature operating profitability after 2030. Dilution, certain contractual cash flows, and financing requirements remain incompletely modeled.

The current results should therefore be interpreted as conditional scenario analysis rather than precise intrinsic-value estimates.

My integrated DCF produces a preliminary enterprise-value range of approximately -$1.24 billion to -$0.97 billion. However, the downside case fails its financing constraints, and these results do not represent common-share prices.

My earlier standalone DCF produced an illustrative sensitivity range of $1.13–$10.26 per share, but it used different operating assumptions and was not linked to the final pro-forma. I therefore do not treat this as the final valuation range or average the two methods. The disagreement highlights how sensitive MP's valuation is to revenue growth, future profitability, and capital requirements.

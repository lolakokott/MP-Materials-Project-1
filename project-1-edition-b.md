## 1. Initial Research and Understanding (Edition A)

In Edition A, my research focused on understanding MP Materials' business operations, financial position, and major risks. I examined the company's transition from primarily producing rare-earth materials toward developing a domestic supply chain that includes metal, alloy, and permanent magnet manufacturing.

My initial research highlighted the potential opportunities associated with MP's expansion, including its growing Magnetics segment, partnerships with the U.S. government, and importance to domestic rare-earth production. However, I also identified several financial concerns. Despite reporting $224.4 million in revenue in 2025, MP generated negative operating cash flow and continued to make substantial investments in its facilities.

At this stage, I had established the company's strengths and risks, but I had not yet determined whether its financial outlook justified an investment at the prevailing market valuation.

## 2. How My Research Evolved

As I progressed through the valuation exercises, I began to distinguish between MP Materials' long-term business potential and its current financial performance.

My initial company research emphasized the opportunities associated with increasing domestic rare-earth production and expanding magnet manufacturing. However, developing the financial models required me to examine whether these opportunities would translate into sufficient cash flows to support the company's valuation.

One of the most important findings was how sensitive MP's valuation is to future profitability and capital expenditures. Although the company has considerable liquidity and government support, continued investment in production facilities creates uncertainty about when its operations will generate sustainable positive free cash flow.

My analysis also revealed limitations in using comparable companies. MP's negative earnings made a conventional P/E valuation inappropriate. I investigated Idaho Strategic Resources (IDR) as a potential comparable, but differences in the companies' operations and business models limited the usefulness of its valuation multiples. This reinforced the importance of evaluating MP through its own operating forecasts rather than relying on a single peer-derived estimate.

## 3. Financial Modeling and Valuation Findings

I developed a five-year pro-forma model covering 2026 through 2030 and connected its operating forecasts to a discounted cash flow valuation.

Using an 11% weighted average cost of capital and a 3% terminal growth assumption, my integrated DCF produced an illustrative base-case enterprise value of approximately **negative $1,108.79 million**. My downside and upside scenarios generated values of approximately negative $1,244.15 million and negative $973.43 million, respectively.

These negative enterprise values should not be interpreted as negative stock prices. Rather, they indicate that the modeled cash flows and terminal assumptions do not support a positive operating enterprise valuation under the selected scenarios. The results also reveal the limitations of forecasting a company undergoing a capital-intensive operational transition.

A separate, simplified DCF analysis produced positive illustrative per-share estimates. However, its assumptions differed from those of my integrated financial model, so I did not average the results or treat them as directly comparable.

This disagreement strengthened my conclusion that MP's valuation is particularly dependent on assumptions about its future operating performance and cash-flow recovery.

## 4. Sensitivity Analysis and Validation

My sensitivity analysis helped identify how changes in operating assumptions affect projected financial performance.

I tested changes in gross margin and capital expenditures, examining their effects on operating income, free cash flow, cash balances, and financing requirements.

For example, increasing the modeled gross margin from 14.1% to 15.1% improved projected 2026 operating income by approximately $2.30 million and free cash flow by approximately $4.35 million. The model also showed how the improvement affected cash balances and later financing needs.

I used accounting reconciliation checks, changed-input testing, and failure detection to evaluate whether the model behaved as expected. These checks were important because a financial model can produce numerical results without necessarily producing reliable conclusions.

The sensitivity exercises demonstrated that the direction and magnitude of changes in key assumptions matter, and that a valuation conclusion should not rely on a single set of forecasts.

## 5. Final Investment Recommendation

**My final recommendation is WATCH / DEFER.**

My research continues to support the long-term strategic importance of MP Materials and its potential role in domestic rare-earth and magnet production. However, my financial analysis highlighted substantial uncertainty surrounding future profitability, capital spending, financing requirements, and cash-flow generation.

I do not believe the evidence from my current models supports initiating a position at this time. Rather than treating MP's strategic importance as sufficient justification for an investment, I would prefer to see clearer evidence that its expanding operations can produce sustainable positive cash flow.

Evidence that could change my recommendation includes improved operating margins, progress toward commercial-scale magnet production, lower capital spending requirements, and stronger cash-flow performance relative to the assumptions in my forecast.

## 6. Reflection on AI and Independent Judgment

AI tools, including ChatGPT and Codex, assisted with drafting, coding, organizing research, and developing parts of my financial analysis. However, I remained responsible for selecting assumptions, reviewing outputs, checking financial definitions, and interpreting the results.

One of the most important lessons from this project was that an AI-assisted financial model is not automatically a reliable valuation. Outputs must be checked against financial statements, accounting relationships, economic expectations, and the limitations of the assumptions used.

Ultimately, my investment judgment became more evidence-driven. Edition A established the business opportunity and its risks, while Edition B evaluates whether the company's financial outlook currently supports an investment.

**The main evolution in my analysis was moving from understanding MP Materials as a strategically important growth company to evaluating whether its projected cash flows justify an investment today.** That distinction is the foundation of my WATCH / DEFER recommendation.
